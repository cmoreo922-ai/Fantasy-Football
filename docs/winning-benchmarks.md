# What It Actually Takes to Win — Score Benchmarks

Researched Sept 2026 from historical DraftKings results.

## The two finish lines

| Contest | Score needed | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Cash (double-up / 50-50) | **~125 DK pts** most weeks | Beat ~55% of entrants; steady, repeatable |
| Large-field GPP (Milly Maker) | **~235–240 to WIN** (avg since 2020: ~236–239; range ~194–277; Week 1 historically lowest ~194) | Beat 99.999% of entrants; rare, massive |
| GPP min-cash | ~150–165 typically | Roughly doubles your entry; keeps you alive |

## Why a "130-projected" lineup can win a tournament that takes 240

**A projection is the average of thousands of possible outcomes, not a
ceiling.** Every player's week is a probability curve: Gibbs projects ~22
because he scores 12 sometimes and 38 sometimes. A lineup projecting 130 hits
240 in the sliver of outcomes where several players spike **at the same time**.

That's the entire reason GPP lineups are built on **correlation (stacks)**:
- Uncorrelated lineup: 9 players each need an independent career day —
  probabilities multiply against you.
- Stacked lineup: ONE event (a 38-point shootout) simultaneously spikes your
  QB, two of his receivers, and the bring-back. You're betting on one game
  environment, not nine coin flips.

So we don't chase the highest projection in GPPs — we chase the highest
**ceiling density**: lineups whose good outcomes cluster together. Projection
tells you the cash floor; construction tells you the tournament ceiling.

## What winning Milly Maker lineups historically look like

- QB + 2–3 teammates from a high-total game, plus a bring-back
- 2–4 players under 10% ownership (the field can't share the prize)
- A salary-saving value whose role was underpriced (our Lloyd-type play)
- Usually 1–2 "why did he do that" picks that hit — i.e., variance embraced,
  not eliminated

## The honest math (read this every week)

- One Milly Maker entry is a massive-upside, tiny-probability ticket even for
  the best players in the world. Pros grind small edges over hundreds of
  entries and full seasons; most of their profit comes from cash games and
  small-field GPPs, with the Milly as the lottery ticket on top.
- The plan that compounds: cash games pay the bankroll, bankroll grows,
  GPP entry count grows, and the tournament tickets stay a fixed small slice.
  The plan that busts: sizing entries to the dream instead of the bankroll.
- Track everything in bankroll/2026-log.md. The numbers, not the feelings,
  tell us if we have an edge.
