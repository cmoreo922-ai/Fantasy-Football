# NFL Coaching Landscape 2026 — Fantasy Impact

Coaching drives the two things fantasy cares about most: **play volume/pace**
and **run-pass split (PROE)**. When a coordinator changes, player values change
even if the roster doesn't. 2026 saw historic turnover: **~21 teams have a new
offensive coordinator**, and 7 teams replaced HC + OC + DC entirely.

> Snapshot researched Sept 2026 (pre–Week 1). Verify current play-caller status
> before relying on this in-season — play-calling duties get stripped/changed
> midseason. Update this doc as the season reveals real tendencies.

## Key 2026 changes and what they mean

- **Chicago (Ben Johnson, year 2 calling plays):** continuity is the story —
  fourth OC since 2023 but Johnson still calls it. His Lions offenses finished
  top-5 in points and yards three straight years. Trust the pass-catcher tree.
- **Baltimore (OC Declan Doyle, from Ben Johnson's Chicago staff):** more 11
  personnel, more under-center — Johnson-tree design grafted onto the Ravens.
  Watch early-season usage closely; scheme-change teams are mispriced in DFS.
- **Arizona (OC Mike LaFleur, McVay tree):** expect motion, condensed
  formations, heavy play-action tying run and pass together — historically
  friendly to a clear WR1 and to RB receiving work.
- **Washington:** far more under-center, fewer option runs and quick throws —
  fundamental scheme change; re-evaluate every skill player's role vs 2025.
- **Buffalo (Joe Brady elevated to HC after McDermott firing):** offense
  largely unchanged — continuity keeps Allen's supporting cast values stable.
- **LA Chargers & Las Vegas:** new Shanahan-tree callers (replacing Greg
  Roman / Chip Kelly designs) — expect wide-zone run games, YAC-friendly
  passing; big scheme upgrades if O-lines hold up.

## How to read a coaching change (the general framework)

1. **Coaching tree predicts scheme.**
   - *Shanahan/McVay tree:* outside zone, motion, play-action, condensed sets →
     one featured RB (great for fantasy), YAC receivers, efficient QB play.
   - *Ben Johnson tree:* creative personnel, screens, downfield shots off run
     looks → concentrated targets to the top 2–3 options.
   - *Air-raid/spread guys:* pace + pass volume → everything gets more targets.
2. **PROE follows the caller, not the roster.** A new pass-happy caller can add
   60+ targets to an offense season-over-season; a run-first caller can delete
   a WR2's value overnight.
3. **Pace changes = plays changes.** More plays = more fantasy points for
   everyone. Track seconds/snap in neutral situations early in the season.
4. **New-staff teams are mispriced in September.** DFS salaries and ownership
   lag real usage changes by 2–4 weeks. The biggest early-season edges are
   players whose role grew under a new scheme before pricing catches up.
5. **Midseason changes matter too.** OC firings and play-caller switches
   (usually after Week 8-ish for struggling teams) reset tendencies — treat
   the first game after a change as high-variance, then re-baseline.

## What to track in-season (per team)

- Neutral-situation pass rate (PROE) and pace (rolling 3 weeks)
- Personnel groupings (11 vs 12 personnel %) — tells you if 2 TEs or 3 WRs see
  the field
- Red-zone play calling (pass vs run inside the 10)
- RB rotation pattern (hot hand vs fixed workhorse vs scripted committee)
- 2-minute / hurry-up personnel (who's on the field when it matters)
