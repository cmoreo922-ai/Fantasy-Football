# Injury Risk — Evaluating Whether a Player Is Worth the Risk

Real money rides on availability. This doc covers (a) what the research says
about injury types and recurrence, (b) a risk-scoring framework, and (c) the
2026 return-from-injury watch list.

## What the research says (NFL-specific where possible)

### Hamstrings (the fantasy killer)
- NFL 2009–2020 study: of ~2,075 hamstring injuries, **38% were recurrences**;
  **12% re-injured in the same season**.
- A previous hamstring injury raises re-injury risk **~2.7×**; a same-season
  prior raises it **~5×**.
- Players returning from hamstrings show reduced high-speed running and reduced
  snaps for the **first few games back** — even "active" isn't full value.
- Rule of thumb: a WR/RB in his first 1–2 games back from a hamstring is a
  **cash-game fade**; GPP-only at reduced expectation, and only if price/
  ownership compensate.

### ACL
- Year-1-back players typically return to availability before returning to
  explosiveness; full form often arrives the *second* season back.
- Complexity matters: multi-structure repairs (ACL + meniscus, follow-up
  procedures) extend the timeline (2026 example: Malik Nabers — ACL + lateral
  meniscus repair + later cyclops lesion excision).
- A second ACL on the same knee (Jonathon Brooks) is a materially worse bet.

### Achilles
- Historically the worst skill-position injury: even after return, expect a
  dip in burst/explosiveness in year 1 (2026 examples: George Kittle,
  Anthony Richardson — Richardson's value is rushing-dependent, so discount
  heavily until the burst shows up on film).

### Other patterns
- **Soft-tissue in camp** (calf/hamstring, e.g., Christian Kirk 2026) →
  elevated early-season re-injury odds.
- **High-ankle sprains:** 4–8 weeks realistic despite optimistic tags; players
  rushed back underperform.
- **Concussions:** protocol timing is unpredictable — avoid in cash until
  cleared with full practice.
- **RB workload/age:** risk climbs with age 27+ and heavy career touches;
  300+ touch seasons predict next-year injury/decline. Matters for season-long
  more than single-week DFS, but explains why old workhorses get hurt mid-slate.

## Player risk score (use before every roster decision)

Score each: 0 = clean, 1 = caution, 2 = red flag.

| Factor | What to check |
|---|---|
| Current designation | Full practice Fri = 0; limited all week = 1; DNP Fri/GTD = 2 |
| Injury type | Bone bruise/rest = 0; joint = 1; soft-tissue (esp. hamstring) = 2 |
| Recency | >6 weeks since return = 0; 3–6 = 1; first 2 games back = 2 |
| History | No recurrence history = 0; prior same-site injury = 2 |
| Role protection | Bell cow/alpha (plays through) = 0; committee (easy to bench mid-game) = 2 |

**0–2 = playable anywhere. 3–4 = GPP-only, need price/ownership discount.
5+ = fade** (the beneficiary is usually the better play).

Key asymmetry: in **cash**, any real risk is disqualifying — a 75%-snaps stud
at full price is worse than a healthy mid-tier player. In **GPP**, a
questionable stud whose ownership collapses can be a leverage play *if* he's
truly expected to play his full role.
