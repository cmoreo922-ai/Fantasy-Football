# DFS Strategy — Cash Games, GPPs, Stacking, Leverage, Bankroll

The single most important idea in DFS: **cash games and tournaments (GPPs) are
different sports.** Know which one you're playing before you build a lineup.

## Cash games (50/50s, Double-Ups, Head-to-Heads)

Goal: beat ~half the field. You need a **high floor**, not a high ceiling.

- Play the highest-projected, safest points per dollar. Chalk is fine — if a
  great play is 60% owned, you *want* him, because being different only hurts you.
- Prioritize **locked-in volume**: RBs with 15+ touches and passing-game work,
  WRs with 25%+ target share, QBs with rushing floors.
- Avoid TD-or-bust players (deep threats, non-target TEs, goal-line-only backs).
- Little to no stacking — stacking adds variance, and variance is your enemy here.
- Avoid injury-risk situations (questionable tags, committee backfields).

## GPPs / tournaments (Milly Maker, etc.)

Goal: beat 99.9% of the field. You need **ceiling and differentiation** — a
lineup that, when everything hits, nobody else has.

- **Your score only matters relative to the field.** A 180-pt lineup wins
  nothing if 5,000 people have the same one.
- **Ownership vs. optimal**: the core math of leverage. If a player is in the
  optimal lineup 15% of the time but rostered by 30% of the field, he's a bad
  bet even though he's good. If he's optimal 15% and owned 5%, he's a great
  bet. Fade over-owned chalk; embrace under-owned ceiling.
- **Don't be contrarian for its own sake.** The goal is *under-owned ceiling*,
  not bad players. Eating some chalk is fine when the chalk is truly elite;
  differentiate elsewhere.

### Stacking (the core GPP tool)

Fantasy points correlate: when a QB throws for 350 and 3 TDs, his receivers
scored those points too.

- **Primary stack:** QB + 1–2 pass catchers from the same team.
- **Bring-back / game stack:** add a pass catcher from the *opposing* team.
  A shootout lifts both sides; the bring-back captures it.
- Target games with **high totals (48+) and close spreads** for game stacks.
- **Negative correlation to avoid:** your QB with the opposing DST; two RBs
  from the same backfield; a QB with his own team's grind-it-out RB is only a
  weak positive (TD-dependent).
- RB + own DST is a sneaky *positive* correlation (winning script = run clock
  + defense on the field with a lead).

### GPP roster construction heuristics (DK Classic)

- Spend up where ceiling lives: elite RB/WR studs win tournaments.
- Find 1–2 **value plays** ($4,000–$5,500 players thrust into big roles by
  injury) — they're what let you afford the studs. The best value plays are
  backup RBs who just inherited a starting job.
- Punting DST ($2,500–$3,200) and TE is often correct to fund studs.
- In large-field GPPs, it's fine (often necessary) to have 2–3 sub-10% owned
  players. In single-entry / small fields, you need less leverage — play closer
  to the optimal.

## Showdown (single-game) strategy

- The **Captain seat decides the contest.** Think "who is the single highest
  scorer in this game?" not "who is the best value?" Ceiling > value at CPT.
- Workhorse RBs are often better in FLEX than CPT; WRs/QBs with spike-week
  potential make better captains. Low-owned CPT choices (a team's WR2, a TE)
  are where leverage lives.
- Build around a **game narrative**: "Team A jumps ahead, Team B throws to
  catch up" → Team A RB + DST, Team B QB + 2 WRs.
- Don't forget kickers and DSTs exist in Showdown — a kicker in a
  field-goal-heavy script is a legitimate FLEX play.
- At least 1 player from each side is required; 5-1 builds are contrarian,
  4-2 and 3-3 are standard.

## Bankroll management (non-negotiable)

- Risk **no more than 5–10% of your bankroll on a single slate**; 1–5% if
  you're conservative or new.
- Sensible split while learning: **~70–80% of slate money in cash games,
  20–30% in GPPs.** Cash pays the bills; GPPs buy the lottery ticket.
- Cap any single large-field GPP entry at 1–2% of bankroll.
- **Contest selection is an edge in itself:** single-entry and 3-max
  tournaments protect you from pros running 150 lineups. As a newer player,
  live in single-entry GPPs and double-ups.
- Track every entry (date, contest, buy-in, result, lineup). Review monthly.

## The late-swap edge (DK-specific)

DK locks players at their individual game times, not slate start. If news
breaks at 3:00 (inactive, weather), you can swap unlocked players. Leave
flexibility in late-game salary when possible, and always check your lineups
between the early and late windows.
