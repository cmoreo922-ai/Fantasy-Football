## Process lessons (carry into Week 2)

1. **Never put 60% of a portfolio on one player whose role we've flagged as a
   committee.** I wrote down the OC's "running back by committee" quote and rode
   Lloyd at 18/30 anyway. The flag and the exposure have to agree — if a risk is
   real enough to write down, it is real enough to cap at ~8/30.
   **Confirmed by the PFF snap counts** (data/pff/2026/parsed/wk01-GB-offense.csv):
   Lloyd 30 snaps / 13 run snaps, **Chris Brooks 38 snaps** — the backup
   out-snapped him. Our "15+ touch" projection was never close.
   *Rule going forward: no player above 8/30 without confirmed bell-cow snaps
   from a game we have already graded.*
2. **Big spreads are a script assumption, not a floor.** An 11.5-point favorite
   losing outright is exactly the tail that kills stacked correlation. Cap any
   single game-script thesis (RB + WR + DST of the same favorite) at a fraction
   of the portfolio.
3. **Conviction and exposure were inverted.** Our correct reads (CIN/TB, the
   fades) were right but modestly sized; our wrong reads were huge. Next week:
   size to the *confidence*, and when a game gets a "sneaky" tag, either back it
   properly or drop it.
4. **Totals were systematically too low.** Six of twelve games beat their total,
   several by 15+. Revisit whether we're shading implied totals down.
5. **STALE-UNIT ERROR — the big methodological one.** We built matchup calls on
   2025 *unit* grades without checking whether the personnel still existed.
   The clearest case: the Achane recommendation rested on LV's 2025 secondary
   (Devin White 105 targets allowed, Pola-Mao 30.1 coverage, Chinn 135.1 rating,
   Kyu Blu Kelly, Darnay Holmes). **Every one of those players is off the 2026
   roster.** LV's actual Week 1 defense graded Chinn 90.7, Maxx Crosby 90.5,
